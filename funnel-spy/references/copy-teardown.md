# Copy teardown — how the money pages persuade

Run this on the **money pages** of every walk: the primary opt-in, the core
sales page, and the checkout. It turns "nice landing page" into a structured
read of *why* it converts — and it's what the CONVERT gate score is justified
with. Capture short verbatim quotes only (headlines, CTAs, guarantee lines);
summarize everything else.

## 1. Awareness level

Which of the five classic awareness stages does the page assume the visitor is
at? **Unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most-aware.**
A page opening with the product's name and a discount assumes most-aware; a
page opening with a symptom story assumes problem-aware.

Then check the match: where does their *traffic* actually come from? Cold
short-form viewers hitting a most-aware page is a leak; a warm email list
hitting a problem-aware explainer is friction. **Mismatch between traffic
temperature and page awareness is one of the most valuable findings this
skill can produce** — call it out whenever you see it.

## 2. The hook

Classify the headline's mechanism (one or two of):

- **Big promise** — a specific outcome, ideally with a number and a timeframe
- **Identity call-out** — names the reader ("For agency owners who…")
- **Enemy** — positions against a villain (the old way, the gurus, the
  algorithm)
- **Curiosity gap** — withholds the mechanism
- **New mechanism** — names a proprietary method/system
- **Social proof lead** — opens with the result count or a marquee win
- **Story lead** — opens in narrative

Note specificity: "$10K/mo in 90 days" vs "grow your business". Vague hooks on
an otherwise strong page usually mean the offer converts on proof, not promise.

## 3. Offer presentation

- **The stack** — is the offer presented as components with assigned values
  ("total value $4,982")? List the components.
- **Anchoring** — strike-through prices, "value" framing, a decoy tier that
  exists to make the real tier look cheap. Name the anchor and the intended
  choice.
- **Price form** — charm pricing (`$497` vs `$500`), payment plans (count and
  markup vs pay-in-full), currency localization.
- **Bonuses** — how many, whether they expire, and whether the bonuses
  outweigh the core offer in claimed value (a classic close pattern — note it).

## 4. Risk reversal

None / standard refund window / **conditional** guarantee ("do the work,
show us, get double") / outrageous guarantee. Quote the guarantee line
verbatim — it's swipe-file material and a direct read on their confidence.

## 5. Urgency — and whether it's real

Classify every urgency element: **real event** (cohort start date, live
cart-close), **real inventory** (verifiable seat cap), **evergreen
countdown** (cross-check the Deadline Funnel / EverWebinar detections from
tech-stack.md — if present, say plainly that the deadline is per-visitor
automation), or **naked claim** ("only 3 left" with nothing behind it).
The urgency type is a character read on the whole funnel.

## 6. Proof density

Count, don't vibe: testimonials (named+faced vs anonymous), case studies with
numbers, logos, member/student counts, ratings, screenshots of results. Note
proof *specificity* (a dollar figure with a name beats ten anonymous quotes)
and proof *placement* (above the fold? beside the price? at the objection
points?).

## 7. Objection handling

FAQ present? A "who this is NOT for" section? Comparison table against
alternatives? Time-commitment and "will this work for me" answered? Absences
are findings.

## 8. The CTA line

Count the CTAs, quote the button verbatim, note first-person framing ("Get my
plan" vs "Submit"), whether one appears above the fold, and how many clicks
sit between the CTA and money.

## Output

One compact block per money page, appended to that page's walk-log entry:

```
COPY READ — [url]
Awareness: [stage] · traffic match: [match / MISMATCH — why]
Hook: [type(s)] — "[headline verbatim]"
Offer: [stack? anchor? plans?] · Guarantee: [type] — "[line]"
Urgency: [type — real / evergreen (Deadline Funnel detected) / naked]
Proof: [counts by type, specificity note]
Objections: [handled how / absent]
CTA: "[button]" ×N
Steal: [the one move on this page worth copying]
```

The `Steal:` lines across all pages are the raw material for Steal This Week.
